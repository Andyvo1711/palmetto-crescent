Harmony Nails & Spa recently received a review from a customer who booked pedicures for two at a specific time. She did the right thing by calling ahead, arrived on schedule, and was seated right away. What happened next turned a well-planned visit into a frustrating one, and it offers a useful lesson for any salon that takes appointments.

## When the Appointment Time and the Service Time Do Not Match

According to the customer, she and her partner were brought to their seats at the designated time, and the foot baths were filled. Then nothing moved forward. About thirty-five minutes later, they were still sitting there, waiting for a technician to begin.

The core issue is not that a salon can run behind. Busy days happen, walk-ins arrive, and a service can take longer than planned. The issue is that the guests had been welcomed in, seated, and started on the first step, which signaled that service was about to begin. When that signal is not followed by action, the wait feels longer and the promise of the appointment feels weaker. A good habit is to seat a guest only when a technician is truly ready to start. If the salon is running behind, it is far better to ask the guests to wait comfortably in the lobby and offer a realistic time than to have them sit with their feet already in the water.

## Cold Water and Silence at the Seat

The customer also noted that the water had gone cold while they waited. A foot bath is meant to be part of a relaxing experience, so a cold bath is a small detail that quietly says the guests were forgotten. Nobody checked in, nobody offered a refresh, and nobody explained what was happening.

This is an easy area to improve. A front desk team member or a lead technician can make a short walk through the pedicure area every ten minutes or so, looking for guests who have been waiting. A quick "I am sorry for the delay, your technician will be with you in about ten minutes, may I warm up your bath?" would likely have changed the tone of the whole visit. A fresh cup of water or tea can also go a long way.

## Turning "We Are Busy" Into a Helpful Answer

When the customer asked how much longer the wait would be, and why they had been encouraged to book an appointment if no one could say, the response was simply that the salon was busy. In her words, "I thought that's what an appointment was for." That line is worth reading carefully, because it captures a fair expectation: an appointment is a shared commitment about time.

"We are busy" may be true, but on its own it does not answer the question the guest actually asked, which was how long. A better reply acknowledges the delay, offers a sincere apology, gives an honest estimate, and, if possible, adds a small gesture such as a discount on an add-on or a complimentary upgrade. The customer ended her review by saying the salon had "just lost a good customer," which is a reminder that one unanswered question can cost a long-term relationship.

## Editorial Perspective

This case is a good reminder that guests judge a salon not only by the quality of the service but by how respected their time feels. Three practical lessons stand out.

First, seat guests only when a technician is ready to begin. If the schedule is running behind, invite guests to relax in the waiting area and give them an honest estimate. Second, check on waiting guests regularly, and refresh foot baths or offer a drink if the wait stretches on. Third, train the front desk and technicians to answer "how much longer?" with a specific time, an apology, and a small gesture of goodwill instead of a general explanation.

With these habits in place, a delay does not have to end in a lost customer. Often, a warm and honest word at the right moment is enough to keep a guest coming back.
