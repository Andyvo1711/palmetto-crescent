A recent review of Angel Nails Mt Pleasant describes a first visit that turned into a week of return trips. The customer, who said she does not usually leave critical reviews, booked an appointment for the first time and hoped for a smooth start. Instead, she described long waits, nails that did not hold up, and a growing sense that she was not welcome. It is a useful case because both problems, the waiting and the durability, are fixable with a few clear habits.

## Waiting Without a Welcome

The customer reported that she arrived for her appointment and waited more than half an hour, and that no one acknowledged her even though it was clear she had walked in. In her words, she was simply hoping someone would say "hello." When she returned to have her nails repaired, she described sitting for another forty-five minutes or more. On her third trip, she called ahead, was told the wait would be about thirty minutes, arrived thirty-five minutes later, and still waited.

Each wait on its own may have a reasonable explanation, but together they left the customer feeling that her time was not planned for. The simplest fix is a greeting within the first minute of anyone walking in, even if it is only "Welcome, we will be right with you." For repair visits, the front desk can give a realistic estimate over the phone and, when possible, set aside a short slot for quick fixes so that a client returning with a problem is not placed at the back of the line. The customer also noted that the technician seemed frustrated with her, which is a reminder that a returning client with a concern deserves a calm and patient reception.

## Nails That Did Not Hold Up

Within a day of her first visit, the customer said three nails cracked and the clear coat began peeling on two others. She returned, and according to her, the technician acknowledged the polish was not performing well and repaired the nails. Then, in her account, the clear coat started peeling again, two more nails broke, and another cracked, all within the first week. She stressed that she is not rough with her hands and said the experience was frustrating. She also said the nails did not look natural, and that she was told they needed to be made thicker for her, an explanation she did not find convincing.

Whatever the cause, early breakage is a strong signal to pause and review the process. A salon can check the product batch and expiration dates, confirm that nails are properly prepped and cured between layers, and review the thickness of the application with the technician. When a client asks why a certain choice is being made, a short and honest explanation helps, for example, "A slightly thicker base gives your natural nails more support, and here is how we keep it looking natural."

## Turning Repeat Visits Into Recovery

By the end of her review, the customer added that another nail had broken and one more was starting to lift. She said she would not return a fourth time in one week. That update captures the real cost of an unresolved issue: the client stopped asking for help and simply left. A clear service guarantee, such as a free repair window within a week, along with a full redo or refund once a second repair is needed, gives staff a simple script and gives the client confidence that the salon stands behind its work.

## Editorial Perspective

The customer summed it up in one line: "This should not be happening to brand new nails." Three practical lessons come from this case.

First, greet every guest promptly and give honest wait times, especially for repair visits. Second, treat early breakage as a prompt to check products, preparation, and technique, and explain choices clearly to the client. Third, set a simple guarantee for repairs so that the second visit is a chance to rebuild trust rather than a repeat of the first problem.

Clients who feel welcomed and supported are often willing to give a salon another chance, even after a rough start.
