A customer shared a review of her visit to JD Nails that centers on a timing problem more than a single technique problem. She came in for a service ahead of her birthday and an upcoming week away, and she says she left unhappy with how her nails looked. Her account is a useful reminder that when a service goes wrong, the way the salon responds in the next few minutes often matters as much as the original mistake.

## A Product That Would Not Set

The customer says that while she was in the chair, the technician could not get the powder to dry. She guesses that the product may have been the cause, and she says her nails looked poor when she left. Powder systems depend on fresh, properly stored product, clean brushes, and the right application thickness, so when a set refuses to dry or harden, the cause may be old product, moisture, or an uneven application.

The best response in that moment is to pause and look at the problem together with the client. A technician can explain what is happening, switch to a fresh container or a different system, and decide with the client whether to continue or redo the set. Checking the product at the start of the day and replacing anything that has been open too long helps prevent the issue from showing up in the middle of an appointment.

## Fixing the Problem Before She Leaves

The customer says she asked to be fit in for a repair, or for the technician to stay a little later to fix her nails, and that this was not offered. She told the salon that her flight was at 10, and she says the only option presented was to come back at 11, which did not work with her travel plans. She adds that she was not given a refund and expects to pay another salon to fix the nails before her birthday.

Salons cannot always stretch their schedules, and staff have limits on their time. Still, a small amount of flexibility often costs far less than losing a client. An open slot earlier in the day, a quick repair at the end of a shift, or a voucher toward a future visit shows the client that her concern was taken seriously. A clear, written fix policy, for example a free repair within a set number of days, also helps staff respond consistently without having to improvise under pressure.

The customer also deserves credit for planning ahead. She communicated her flight time and her need for a quick turnaround, which gave the salon what it needed to find a workable solution.

## Editorial Perspective

This account highlights three practical lessons. First, treat a product that will not dry as a signal to stop and reassess, rather than continuing to the end of the service, and keep product fresh and well stored. Second, listen carefully when a client shares her schedule, since a travel date or a special event changes what a good fix looks like, and look for options that fit her timeline. Third, make sure every team member knows the salon's repair and refund policy, and encourage them to offer a goodwill gesture when a result falls short. A client who feels heard before a trip or a special occasion is far more likely to return.
